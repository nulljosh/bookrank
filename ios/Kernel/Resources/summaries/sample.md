# The Art of War: a sample

# Chapter 1: Know the ground
Sun Tzu opens with a plain claim. War is serious, so you plan before you move. Count what you have, count what the other side has, and look at the weather, the terrain and the mood of your people.

Most battles are decided before they start. The side that prepares more usually wins, and often without fighting at all.

# Chapter 2: Win without a fight
The best general breaks the enemy's plans, not his army. Cities that fall without a siege cost nothing. Every long campaign drains the treasury and the patience of the people at home.

So speed matters, and so does knowing when to stop. A clever peace beats an expensive victory.

# Chapter 3: Be like water
Water avoids high ground and runs to the low places. An army should do the same: avoid strength, strike weakness, and change shape to fit what the enemy does.

There is no fixed way to win. Read the situation, stay flexible, and let your opponent make the first mistake.
